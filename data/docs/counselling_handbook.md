# NEET-UG Counselling Handbook (SYNTHETIC DEMO EDITION)

This handbook is a simplified, synthetic summary written for the NEET.info demo
assistant. It follows the general shape of public counselling information
brochures but it is NOT an official document. Rules, amounts and dates below
are illustrative only.

## 1. Quotas covered in this dataset

- **All India Quota (AIQ):** 15% of MBBS and BDS seats in government medical
  colleges are filled through central counselling. Candidates from any state
  can apply for AIQ seats. No domicile certificate is needed for AIQ seats.
- **State Quota:** the remaining 85% of seats in government colleges, and the
  seats of private colleges in this dataset, are filled through state
  counselling. Only candidates with a domicile certificate of that state can
  take a State Quota seat in that state.
- **Deemed University seats:** seats in deemed universities are filled through
  central counselling and are open to candidates from all states. In this
  dataset they are listed under one open category (GEN).

NRI quota, management quota, PwD sub-quotas, AIIMS and JIPMER seats are not
part of this dataset.

## 2. Categories

The dataset uses five categories: GEN (unreserved), EWS (economically weaker
section), OBC (other backward classes, non-creamy layer), SC (scheduled castes)
and ST (scheduled tribes). A reserved-category candidate must upload a valid
category certificate at the time of registration. A candidate who cannot
produce the certificate at reporting is treated as GEN and may lose the
allotted seat.

## 3. Eligibility

To be eligible for counselling a candidate must have qualified NEET-UG. The
qualifying percentile is 50th for GEN and EWS candidates and 40th for OBC, SC
and ST candidates.

## 4. Rounds

Counselling in this dataset has two main rounds:

1. **Round 1:** registration, fee payment, choice filling and locking, seat
   allotment, and reporting to the allotted college.
2. **Round 2:** fresh registration is allowed; candidates who joined in
   Round 1 may opt for an upgrade. Closing ranks in Round 2 are usually higher
   (less competitive) than in Round 1 because vacant seats are re-offered.

Mop-up and stray-vacancy rounds happen after Round 2 in real counselling, but
their closing ranks are not included in this dataset.

## 5. What "opening rank" and "closing rank" mean

The **opening rank** is the best NEET All India Rank (AIR) that was allotted a
seat in a given college, course, quota, category and round. The **closing
rank** is the last (highest-numbered) AIR allotted a seat in that same group.
A lower number is a better rank. A closing rank from a previous year is a
reference point only: it changes every year with the number of seats, the
number of candidates and their choices. It is never a guarantee of admission.

## 6. Security deposit and free exit (illustrative)

- Registration for AIQ and deemed counselling requires a refundable security
  deposit: INR 10,000 for GEN candidates and INR 5,000 for SC, ST and OBC
  candidates applying only to government seats; INR 2,00,000 for candidates who
  include deemed-university seats in their choices.
- A candidate who does not join an allotted Round 1 seat, or who resigns it
  before the Round 1 reporting deadline, may participate in Round 2 without
  losing the deposit ("free exit").
- A candidate who joins a Round 2 seat and later resigns forfeits the security
  deposit and cannot take part in further rounds.

## 7. Documents needed at reporting

1. NEET-UG admit card and score card
2. Provisional allotment letter
3. Class 10 certificate (date of birth proof)
4. Class 10+2 mark sheet and passing certificate
5. Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN, passport, voter ID or driving licence)
6. Eight passport-size photographs
7. Category certificate (if applicable)
8. Domicile certificate (State Quota seats only)

## 8. Choosing between government, private and deemed colleges

Government colleges in this dataset charge much lower annual tuition than
private and deemed colleges (see fees_2025.csv). Private and deemed colleges
usually close at much higher (less competitive) ranks. The choice depends on
the candidate's rank, category, domicile, family budget and other personal
factors. The NEET.info assistant can list options from the data, but a
counsellor and the candidate's family must make the final decision.
